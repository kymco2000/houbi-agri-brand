# Graduate Research Workflow

Use this skill automatically when the user asks to find papers or academic sources; explore a thesis topic or research direction; prepare a literature review; read, compare, deconstruct, or organize papers/PDFs; or draft, revise, structure, or cite a thesis or proposal.

Do not use this as a substitute for a course-specific workflow when the user is handling recordings, lecture notes, or coursework that has its own scope.

## Workflow

Choose only the stages the request needs:

1. Scope the question: topic, discipline, population/object, language, date range, and needed evidence.
   When selecting or comparing research topics, apply the topic-feasibility module in [references/topic-and-evidence.md](references/topic-and-evidence.md). Produce a feasible next verification step before recommending commitment to a topic; keep missing data access and resources explicit.
2. Discover candidate sources in suitable academic databases and repositories. Use Connected Papers or ResearchRabbit to expand from verified seed papers; treat their networks as discovery leads.
3. Verify citation metadata against a publisher page, DOI/Crossref record, or university repository. Capture a DOI or stable URL when available.
4. Use Zotero for citation-library import, tagging, and deduplication when it is available and the user wants library management.
5. Analyze only full texts the user supplied or is authorized to access. Distinguish source text from an AI-generated summary.
6. For a literature review or proposal, make a claim–source audit before drafting or judging it. For each consequential claim, record its location in the draft, the cited original source and page/section, what that source actually supports, evidence level, and disposition: supported, narrower than stated, contradicted, or unverified. Check whether a proposed research gap follows from the compared literature; a lack of citations is not proof of novelty.
   Maintain the evidence matrix in [references/topic-and-evidence.md](references/topic-and-evidence.md) as the input to this audit. Reuse existing project matrices and IDs rather than creating a competing record. Use the provided templates when no suitable project format exists.
7. Produce the requested evidence-bounded deliverable: search strategy, candidate table, annotated bibliography, comparison, paper deconstruction, theory map, research flow, proposal review, outline, or draft. In a proposal review, also identify unverified data access, parameters, methods, validation, and feasibility assumptions separately from literature errors.
8. Store durable summaries, reusable methods, and source indexes in the registered Obsidian vault only when the user asks to save or sync to the knowledge base.

## Evidence and authorization gates

- Never invent a citation, DOI, author, journal, result, or quotation.
- Label substantive conclusions as **full-text evidence**, **abstract/metadata**, or **inference**. State source limits when a full text was not reviewed. Keep AI summaries and the researcher's interpretation separate from source findings.
- A candidate list is not a completed literature review. Do not overstate coverage, recency, or evidence quality.
- Do not use a logged-in account, upload private material, accept terms, download restricted material, send material, or publish without direct authorization or a clearly active authorized session.
- Respect copyright and database terms. Preserve user-supplied originals and keep course material separated by course.

## Output standard

Make the response proportionate to the request. Include search scope and sources for discovery work; include source identifiers beside factual research claims; distinguish verified facts from suggested next steps. Before drafting a literature review or research proposal, inventory the available sources and flag missing verification.

For the user's curated tool notes and media resources, read [references/resource-map.md](references/resource-map.md) when the task needs tool selection, source-management advice, or learning resources. Its links are aids to navigation, not proof that current tool features or policies remain unchanged.
